Research

The work falls into three streams. Each lists its published papers and working projects.

01

Right and wrong

This work studies moral decision-making: when people choose to be honest or dishonest, whom they are willing to harm, and what leads people to act for the good of others.

Published

  • Revisiting Gino, Ayal, et al.’s (2009) contagion and differentiation in unethical behavior: A registered conceptual replication and extensionJournal of Experimental Social Psychology, 2026
  • Effectiveness of ex ante honesty oaths in reducing dishonesty depends on contentNature Human Behaviour, 2024
  • A Registered Report on Gender Bias in Interpersonal Dishonesty: Are Females and Males Cheated Differently?Social Psychological and Personality Science, 2024
  • Does Conceptual Abstraction Moderate Whether Past Moral Deeds Motivate Consistency or Compensatory Behavior? A Registered Replication and Extension of Conway and Peetz (2012)Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 2024
  • Do honesty-nudges really work? A large-scale field experiment in an insurance contextJournal of Consumer Behaviour, 2021

In progress

  • Business-size bias in consumer dishonestyRevise and Resubmit
  • Intergroup bias in coalitional but not selfish dishonestyUnder Review
  • Recycling rivalry: social identity appeals and recyclingRevise and Resubmit

02

Beliefs and bias

This work compares what people believe about others with how others actually think and behave. It asks when these beliefs are accurate, when they are biased, and what follows when they are wrong.

Published

  • The Morality-Effectiveness Disconnect of Pro-Environmental BehaviorsPersonality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 2026
  • Beliefs versus reality: People overestimate the actual dishonesty of othersJournal of Experimental Social Psychology, 2026
  • Human Is Gold: Why Premium Customers Hate Chatbots and What to Do About ItJournal of Interactive Marketing, 2025

In progress

  • People underestimate the acceptability of canceling plansPreprint

03

Time

This work studies how people think about time, and how they connect who they are now with who they were and who they will become. A recurring finding is that people treat the past and the future differently.

Published

  • Does Self-Projection Have a Dominant Temporal Direction? In Nine Countries, People Feel More Similar to Their Future Than Past SelvesPersonality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 2026
  • The role of social norms in retirement saving: Evidence from two natural field experimentsJournal of Business Research, 2025
  • Not too little and not too much? The relationships between spare time, spare money, and subjective well-beingThe Journal of Positive Psychology, 2025

In progress

  • AI integration timing and creativity judgmentsPreprint
